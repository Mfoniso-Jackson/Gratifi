# Competition: why venues choose others, and why they'd choose GratiFi

**The short version:** QR and NFC tipping with no app is already common, funded and widely used in
the UK and Ireland. GratiFi cannot win on "scan to tip". It has to win on what the others treat as an
extra: **the thank-you itself** (notes, recognition, a record staff keep) and what that does for
keeping staff.

Researched 18 November 2026, mostly from company websites, press coverage and review sites. Company
claims (scale, fees, features) are not independently checked. Where we say a competitor "doesn't
show" a feature, it means we didn't find it, not that it's absent; check before using it in sales.

## Who's out there

| Competitor | Where | What it is | Scale and backing (as reported) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Strikepay** | Ireland and UK | QR and NFC tipping straight to the worker; no app, no venue integration; daily payouts | 33,000 locations and over €10M processed by early 2024; bought UK rival Gratsi |
| **TiPJAR** | UK | Cashless tipping and tronc platform; tap-to-tip devices, links, till integrations; savings tool for staff | Raised £4.5M in March 2026; about 1,000 tips a minute on a busy day |
| **EasyTip** | UK and beyond | QR tipping with tronc management for UK tipping law and HMRC rules | Free for businesses, per its app listing; mixed reviews on fees |
| **Card machines** (Teya, SumUp, Square, Dojo, Clover) | Everywhere | A tip prompt on the card machine at payment | Already in nearly every venue; allocation is left to the venue's own policy |
| **eTip** | US hotels mainly | QR and NFC tipping for housekeeping, bell staff and front desk, plus post-stay links | Used across Davidson Hospitality's 85 hotels; endorsed by BWH Hotel Group |
| **Kickfin, TipHaus** | US restaurants | Back-office tip pooling, calculation and instant payouts to staff | Venture-backed; pricing by quote |
| Others | UK and Europe | Uptip, TipYo, béné, eDrixx and similar QR tipping apps | Smaller |

## Why venues choose them

| Competitor | Why venues pick it |
| --- | --- |
| Card machines | Nothing to add: the prompt is already there, at the moment of paying |
| Strikepay | Set up in minutes, no integration; money goes straight to the worker, so no payroll or tipping-law admin for the owner; daily payouts staff love; known brand in Ireland |
| TiPJAR | Covers the tipping law and tronc for the owner; plugs into tills (for example Zonal); 100% of tips to staff; a UKHospitality supplier; funded and growing |
| EasyTip | Free for the venue; tronc handled |
| eTip | Built for hotels; chain-level deals and endorsements; works across every guest touchpoint |
| Kickfin, TipHaus | Solve the back-office pain (tip pooling maths and payouts), not the tipping moment |

**What they all compete on:** easy set-up, getting the tip to staff, and handling the law and payroll.
The tip itself is the product.

## Where GratiFi is weaker today

- **No scale, brand or funding** against Strikepay and TiPJAR.
- **Slower money for staff:** GratiFi pays the venue, which pays staff through payroll. Strikepay pays
  workers daily. Staff will notice.
- **More set-up for owners:** a Stripe Connect account per venue, versus "sign up in minutes".
- **No till integrations or tronc service** yet.
- **Ireland is Strikepay's home market**, so it's not the easy second step the strategy assumed.

## Why a venue would choose GratiFi instead

GratiFi's case has to rest on recognition and retention, which the competitors we found treat as
secondary:

1. **The thank-you, not just the tip.** A note with every tip, shown to staff and their manager. The
   competitors we found lead with money; customer notes were not prominent in what we saw.
2. **Help keeping staff.** Owners struggle with 52% to 75% yearly turnover. A weekly digest of what
   customers loved, and who was thanked most, gives owners something a tip prompt can't (planned in
   the AI playbook).
3. **A record staff keep.** Staff take their verified history of thanks to their next job. No
   competitor we found offers a portable, checkable record of recognition.
4. **Proof that can be checked.** Each tip is permanently and privately recorded, so the venue can
   show staff, auditors or the tipping-law inspector exactly what was received.
5. **Sponsored staff goals (later).** Brands funding staff dreams with verified impact is unusual in
   this market; it stays off until the legal review.
6. **Honest fees.** Customers can cover the card fee themselves, shown up front, with no hidden
   percentage taken from staff.

## What this changes in the project

1. **Position GratiFi as staff recognition, not "QR tipping".** Pitch: "Your customers already want
   to thank your team. GratiFi turns those thank-yous into notes your staff read and a record they
   keep." Tipping is how it's paid for, not what it's for.
2. **The pilot must prove notes matter.** The "tips with a note" and "staff read their tips weekly"
   numbers now decide whether GratiFi has a reason to exist next to Strikepay and TiPJAR.
3. **Pick pilot venues that don't already use a competitor**, or ask why they left one; both answers
   are gold.
4. **Faster pay to staff is a decision to take after the pilot.** Direct payouts to staff (option B)
   are the norm for Strikepay. Revisit option B for self-employed staff first.
5. **The country order has changed.** Ireland is Strikepay's home market, so the plan is now: UK
   first, then a deeper UK, then the US, with Ireland after that (see the strategy README).
6. **Partner rather than fight on the till.** Card machines own the payment moment; GratiFi should
   live where they don't reach (staff badges, rooms, chairs) and later integrate with them.

## Questions to answer in the pilot

- Has the venue used Strikepay, TiPJAR or EasyTip? Why did they choose it, or not?
- Do staff care more about notes or about getting paid daily?
- Would an owner pay for recognition insights if tipping itself were free elsewhere?
